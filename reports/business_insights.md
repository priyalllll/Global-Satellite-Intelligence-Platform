# Business Insights Report

## Project
Global Satellite Intelligence Platform (GSIP)

### Objective
The objective of this analysis is to examine the global satellite ecosystem by analyzing satellite operators, countries, mission purposes, orbital characteristics, and launch trends. The insights generated support strategic decision-making through data-driven analysis.

---

# Module 1: Business Overview

## Key Findings

- The database contains **7,553 satellites**, providing a comprehensive view of the global satellite industry.
- Satellites are operated by numerous organizations across multiple countries, demonstrating a highly diverse international space ecosystem.
- The dataset spans several decades of satellite launches, enabling historical trend analysis.
- Satellite launch masses and expected operational lifetimes vary significantly across missions, indicating differences in satellite design and objectives.

## Business Value

This overview establishes the scale and diversity of the global satellite industry and provides baseline KPIs for executive dashboards.

---

# Module 2: Country Analysis

## Key Findings

- A small number of countries operate the majority of the world's satellites.
- Some countries have many satellite operators, indicating mature and competitive space industries.
- Several countries continue to launch new satellites, reflecting ongoing investments in space technology.
- Countries with higher satellite counts generally demonstrate stronger participation in commercial and governmental space programs.

## Business Value

Country-level analysis helps identify leading space nations, emerging markets, and opportunities for international collaboration.

---

# Module 3: Operator Analysis

## Key Findings

- A few major organizations manage a large share of the global satellite fleet.
- Some operators focus on specialized missions, while others support multiple mission categories.
- Large operators generally maintain continuous launch activity, indicating long-term operational capabilities.
- Mission diversity varies significantly among operators.

## Business Value

Understanding operator performance helps identify industry leaders, major commercial players, and organizations with diverse space capabilities.

---

# Module 4: Orbit Analysis

## Key Findings

- Low Earth Orbit (LEO) contains the largest concentration of satellites.
- Geostationary Orbit (GEO) remains important for communication and broadcasting services.
- Orbital characteristics such as altitude, inclination, and orbital period differ significantly across orbit classes.
- Countries operating satellites in multiple orbit classes demonstrate broader technical capabilities.

## Business Value

Orbit analysis supports mission planning, congestion assessment, and infrastructure planning for future satellite deployments.

---

# Module 5: Mission Analysis

## Key Findings

- Communications is one of the most common satellite mission categories.
- Earth Observation continues to play an important role in environmental monitoring and defense applications.
- Different mission types have varying average launch masses and operational lifetimes.
- Countries with diverse mission portfolios demonstrate advanced space capabilities.

## Business Value

Mission analysis helps governments, investors, and commercial organizations understand global priorities and technological focus areas.

---

# Overall Conclusion

The Global Satellite Intelligence Platform provides a comprehensive analytical view of the worldwide satellite ecosystem. Through SQL-based business analytics, the project identifies industry leaders, operational trends, mission distributions, and orbital characteristics that support informed strategic decision-making.

Future phases will extend the analysis with launch trends, advanced SQL techniques, interactive Power BI dashboards, and predictive analytics.
